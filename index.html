Legal Defintion

Not guilty by reason of insanity is a plea or affirmative defense by excuse entered by a defendant in a criminal trial, in which the defendant claims that they were so mentally disturbed or incapacitated at the time of the offense that they did not have the required intention or mens rea to commit the crime, and are therefore not guilty. Not guilty by reason of insanity can also be a verdict entered by a jury in a criminal case, stating that the defendant cannot be liable because of the defendant’s insanity. Such a verdict may require the defendant to be admitted into a mental institution. To prove legal insanity, defendants usually put forth expert testimony on their psychological evaluation. Various jurisdictions use various tests to judge such pleas (for example: M’Naghten Rule, cognitive test, irresistible impulse test, substantial capacity test, etc.). (Cornell Law, 2026)

Simplified Defintion

This is a defense that defendants can use in the court of law to help prove they are not guilty of a crime based on a mental disorder/disease that can be upheld by certain professions(pyschologists,etc). Legal standards or tests vary on what they qualify and look for in these cases, from a cognitive (right from wrong) , to even any correlation of mental illness at all, to not being able to control your actions.
