Origin:

The Model Penal Code (MPC) originated as a 10-year drafting project launched in 1952 by the American Law Institute (ALI) to reform, standardize, and modernize chaotic U.S. criminal laws. This states 'Did the defendant lack "substantial capacity" to appreciate the criminality of their act or conform their conduct to the law?'

How its used in the Justice System?:

Defendants are evaluated by two distinct components, addressing both the mind and the will:

  • The Cognitive Prong ("To Appreciate"): This addresses whether the defendant could understand that what they were doing was wrong. By using the word "appreciate" rather than the strict word "know" (found in older standards like the M'Naghten Rule), the MPC allows for emotional and nuanced understanding, rather than just cold, surface-level intellectual knowledge.
  • The Volitional Prong ("To Conform"): This addresses self-control. It acknowledges that a person might intellectually understand that an act is a crime but, due to a severe mental defect, completely lack the ability to override their impulses and choose not to do it

Modern use:

Used in 16 states, often seen as less strict than the M'Naghten Rule especially seen in cases like Hinckley Jr.
