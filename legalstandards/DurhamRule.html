Legal Standards: Durham Rule (Product Test)

[HOME] [ABOUT] [REFERENCES] [CASES] [LEGAL STANDARDS]

Origin:

Durham v. United States (1954): The legal and moral traditions of the western world require that those who, of their own free will and with evil intent .... commit acts which violate the law, shall be criminally responsible for those acts.., where such acts stem from and are the product of a mental disease or defect .... moral blame shall not attach, and hence there will not be criminal responsibility. (Ogloff, 1993) Essientially the product test or Durham rule states that if you commit a crime while having a mental issue (disease,defect, etc) that you are not guilty of said crime you committed.

Modern use:

Currently only still used in New Hampshire. Often considered too broad and overly reliant on expert psychiatric testimony rather than jury judgment.

Main purpose/focal point

The test's main focus is on the causal relationship between mental disease/defect and criminal conduct.
