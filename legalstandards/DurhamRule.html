Origin:

Durham v. United States (1954): The legal and moral traditions of the western world require that those who, of their own free will and with evil intent .... commit acts which violate the law, shall be criminally responsible for those acts.., where such acts stem from and are the product of a mental disease or defect .... moral blame shall not attach, and hence there will not be criminal responsibility. (Ogloff et al., 1993)

Modern Use:

Currently, the Durham-style product test is used only in New Hampshire. Often considered too broad and overly reliant on expert psychiatric testimony rather than jury judgment.

Main Purpose/Focal Point:

The test focuses on whether the defendant's criminal conduct was the product of a mental disease or defect, rather than primarily asking whether the defendant understood that the conduct was wrong.
