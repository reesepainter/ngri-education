Legal Standards:

There are 4 main legal standards we use to help clarify if a defendant is guilty or not guilty by reason of insanity. Those four are:

  • M'Naghten Rule
  • Irresistible Impulse Test
  • Model Penal Code
  • Durham Rule (Product Test)

How Do They Compare:

Research shows that: Four forensic psychiatrists were asked to indicate whether they thought 164 defendants met any or all of four insanity tests: 1) the American Law Institute (ALI) cognitive criterion, 2) the ALI volitional criterion, 3) the APA test, and 4) the M'Naghten rule. RESULTS: The four psychiatrists determined that 97.5% of the defendants met the ALI volitional criterion, 73.9% met the APA criterion, 70.3% met the M'Naghten rule, and 69.5% met the ALI cognitive criterion. Nearly two-thirds of the defendants met all four insanity tests, and 24.4% met only the ALI volitional test. Few defendants met cognitive tests without also meeting the ALI volitional test. Elimination of the volitional test for insanity reduced the rate of psychiatric recommendations of acquittal by 24.4%. CONCLUSIONS: These findings highlight the fact that the primary logical division between volitional and cognitive standards appears to be powerful but that distinctions between types of cognitive standards are not terribly powerful. In addition, the variation among individual raters must be viewed as an important determinant of how any insanity standard is applied. (American Journal of Psychiatry, 1991)

The four different legal standards or tests have different purposes and focal points. The M'Naghten Rule test for if the defendant knew from right and wrong during the incident of the crime (Cognitive), the Irresistible Impulse Test tests for if they had control over their actions (Volitional Focus), Durham Rule(also called the product test) interacts with a more casual relation to mental illness with crime stating that if you were under the effects of some mental disease or defect that you cannot be held responsible for said crime(arguably the least reliable) basically stating if that defendant didn't have the mental illness they had the crime wouldn't have happened, and finally the Model Penal Code is a mix between the M'naghten Rule and the Irresitble Impulse test by having both Cognitive and Volitional prongs. Between the four the most prominent today are the Model Penal Code and the M'Naghten Rule both being used widely throughout the US.
