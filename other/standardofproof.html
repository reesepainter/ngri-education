Definition:

the level of certainty and the degree of evidence necessary to establish proof in a criminal or civil proceeding (Merriam-Webster) or the How much evidence/proof is needed.

Origin:

Before 1984, the federal courts followed the precedent set by the U.S. Supreme Court in Davis v. United States (1895). Once a defendant introduced any basic evidence of insanity, the burden shifted to the prosecution in which they had to prove the defendant was sane beyond a reasonable doubt. It made it easier to secure a NGRI verdict under this standard,

Modern Use:

After 1984's reform act, it's split into two different major categories for the standard of proof: Preponderance of the Evidence and Clear and Convincing Evidence. For preponderance of the evidence that means the defendant must show it is more likely than not, greater than 50% chance ,that they were legally insane at the time of the offense. This is considered the least demanding legal standard of proof and typically used more often in civil cases but can also be seen in certain NGRI cases. While Clear and Convincing Evidence is a higher, more demanding standard used in a minority of states (such as federal courts and anything under the Insanity Defense Reform Act of 1984), requires the defense to prove that the mental illness(disease or defect) makes the insanity claim highly probable.
