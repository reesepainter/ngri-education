Definition:

Guilty but mentally ill (GBMI) is a legal verdict meaning a defendant is convicted of a crime due to them being legal sane at the time of the crime but having a history or diagnoses of a mental illness. Similarily to NGRI rare and often seen in less cases than NGRI but varies by jurisdiction.

Origin:

The legislative purpose of the GBMI law was to find a halfway point between guilty and not guilty by reason of insanity; to have a disturbed mind, but not quite disturbed enough to be totally exculpated. Thus, with that history, the GBMI leg-islation was enacted along with a revised insanity statue. Strict prodecural and legal guidelines were elucidated. (BLUNT & STOCK ,1985)

Modern Use:

Used in 20 states, scholars like Scott Leigh Sherman question the benefits/uses of the verdict including the lack of guaranteed treatment, jury confusion and equivalent jail time. See this link for more information on Sherman's article
